# Index: Other Works of Thomas Aquinas

## Purpose

Beyond the great Summae and Disputed Questions, Thomas produced an enormous body of work: commentaries on Aristotle, commentaries on Scripture, theological treatises, and shorter works (*opuscula*). This index catalogs these for research purposes.

### Catena Aurea (Golden Chain)

**Date**: 1263-1267
**Content**: Commentary on all four Gospels composed of patristic quotations

| Gospel | Dedication |
|--------|------------|
| Matthew | Pope Urban IV |
| Mark | Cardinal Annibald |
| Luke | Cardinal Annibald |
| John | Cardinal Annibald |

The *Catena* strings together quotations from Greek and Latin Fathers, providing a patristic commentary on the Gospels. Valuable for seeing what patristic sources Thomas used.

**Key Feature**: Thomas had Greek texts translated into Latin for this work, providing access to Eastern Fathers previously unavailable in the West.

## Other Commentaries

### Boethius

| Work | Text | Date | Notes |
|------|------|------|-------|
| **In De Trin.** | On the Trinity | ~1258-1259 | Important for science classification |
| **In De Hebd.** | On the Hebdomads | ~1258-1259 | On participation |

**Super Boetium De Trinitate** (Commentary on Boethius' *De Trinitate*):
- Questions 1-2: Division of speculative sciences
- Questions 5-6: Method of theology, philosophy, mathematics, natural science

**Key passage** (In De Trin. q.5, a.3):
> "Natural science treats of things as movable, mathematics of things as quantified, metaphysics of things according to being."

### Pseudo-Dionysius

| Work | Text | Date | Notes |
|------|------|------|-------|

### Peter Lombard

| Work | Text | Date | Notes |
|------|------|------|-------|
| **In Sent.** | Sentences | 1252-1256 | Early major work; 4 books |

The *Commentary on the Sentences* was Thomas' first major theological synthesis, written when he was a young bachelor at Paris. It covers all major theological topics and is often useful for comparison with his later works.

**Structure**:
- Book I: God (Trinity)
- Book II: Creation, Angels, Man
- Book III: Incarnation, Virtues
- Book IV: Sacraments, Last Things

## Theological Treatises (Major Opuscula)

### De Ente et Essentia (On Being and Essence)

**Date**: ~1252-1256 (early Paris period)
**Chapters**: 6
**Significance**: Thomas' earliest metaphysical treatise; foundational for understanding his entire philosophy

| Chapter | Topic |
|---------|-------|
| 1 | Introduction; being and essence |
| 2 | Essence in material substances |
| 3 | Essence related to genus, species, difference |
| 4 | Essence in separate substances (angels) |
| **5** | **Essence in God** (essence = existence) |
| 6 | Accidents and essence |

**Key passage** (DEE c.4):
> "There must be something whose essence is its existence (*cuius essentia sit ipsum suum esse*), or else there would be infinite regress."

**Key passage** (DEE c.5):
> "God alone is pure act... In every other being, essence differs from existence."

### Compendium Theologiae (Compendium of Theology)

**Date**: ~1265-1273 (incomplete)
**Addressee**: Reginald of Piperno
**Structure**: Faith, Hope, Charity (only Faith complete + partial Hope)

A short summary of Christian doctrine organized around the theological virtues.

| Part | Chapters | Content |
|------|----------|---------|
| Faith | 1-246 | Creed exposition (God, Trinity, Incarnation, Last Things) |
| Hope | 1-10 (incomplete) | On prayer, Lord's Prayer |
| Charity | Not written | |

**Key passage** (Comp. c.2):
> "The knowledge of divine things is called wisdom... Human salvation consists in the knowledge of truth."

### De Unitate Intellectus (On the Unity of the Intellect)

**Date**: 1270
**Against**: Siger of Brabant and the "Latin Averroists"
**Content**: Defends individual human intellects against the Averroist theory of a single intellect for all humanity

**Key passage** (De Unitate, c.5):
> "This position is clearly impossible... This man understands: it could not be said if the intellect were not part of him."

### De Aeternitate Mundi (On the Eternity of the World)

**Date**: ~1271
**Topic**: Whether the world's temporal beginning can be philosophically demonstrated
**Position**: It cannot; we know it only by faith

**Key passage**:
> "That the world did not always exist cannot be demonstrated... but is held by faith alone."

### De Substantiis Separatis (On Separate Substances)

**Date**: ~1271-1273 (incomplete)
**Topic**: Angels and separated substances
**Content**: History of opinions, philosophical treatment

### De Regimine Principum (On the Rule of Princes)

**Date**: ~1265-1267 (Book I and partial Book II by Thomas)
**Addressee**: King of Cyprus
**Topic**: Political philosophy, kingship, mixed constitution

**Note**: Books II.5 through IV completed by Ptolemy of Lucca.

**Key passage** (De Reg. I.1):
> "It is natural for man to live in society... He needs someone to direct the community toward the common good."

## Research Tips

1. **De Ente et Essentia**: Start here for Thomas' metaphysics—it's short and foundational
2. **Aristotle commentaries**: These show Thomas the philosopher; compare with ST
3. **Sentences Commentary**: Earlier work, useful for tracking development
4. **Scripture commentaries**: Often neglected but valuable for understanding Thomas the theologian
5. **Date matters**: Early works (In Sent., DEE) may differ from mature works (ST, DQ)
6. **Catena Aurea**: Treasure trove of patristic material as Thomas knew it
7. **Authenticity**: Some works are disputed; check scholarly consensus
8. **Latin is best**: Translations vary; when precision matters, check Latin

## Works by Period

### Early Period (1252-1259)
- In Sent. (Commentary on Sentences)
- De Ente et Essentia
- De Principiis Naturae
- Scripture commentaries (Isaiah, Jeremiah)
- De Veritate

### Italian Period (1259-1268)
- Summa Contra Gentiles
- Catena Aurea
- De Potentia, De Malo
- Compendium Theologiae (begun)
- Commentary on Job
- Summa Theologiae (begun)

### Second Paris Period (1268-1272)
- Aristotle commentaries (most)
- De Unitate Intellectus
- De Aeternitate Mundi
- Continuation of ST
- In De Div. Nom.
- In De Causis

### Final Period (1272-1273)
- Naples teaching
- Scripture commentaries (Romans, John)
- Completion of ST III (partial)
- December 6, 1273: Mystical experience
- "All I have written seems like straw..."
