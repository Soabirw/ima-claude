# Index: Summa Theologiae

## Purpose

The **Summa Theologiae** (1265-1273) is Thomas Aquinas' masterwork—an attempt to present the whole of sacred doctrine in an orderly manner suitable for "beginners" (though these beginners were Dominican theology students). It remains unfinished; Thomas stopped writing on December 6, 1273, after a mystical experience, saying "All I have written seems like straw compared to what has been revealed to me."

This index guides research into the *Summa's* structure and key passages.

---

## Structure Overview

The *Summa* follows a great circle: from God (Prima Pars), through the creature's movement toward God (Secunda Pars), returning to God through Christ (Tertia Pars).

```
         GOD (Prima Pars)
           ↙         ↘
    Creation        Trinity
           ↓
     CREATURES MOVING TO GOD (Secunda Pars)
        ├── Human Acts, Virtues, Law (I-II)
        └── Particular Virtues, States of Life (II-II)
           ↓
     CHRIST THE WAY (Tertia Pars)
        ├── Incarnation
        ├── Life of Christ
        └── Sacraments
```

## Prima Pars (ST I): God and Creation

*Questions 1-119*

### The Nature of Sacred Doctrine (q. 1)

**Q. 1: The nature and extent of sacred doctrine** (10 articles)

| Article | Question Asked | Thomas' Answer |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| 1 | Is sacred doctrine necessary beyond philosophy? | Yes—revelation gives knowledge needed for salvation |
| 2 | Is it a science? | Yes—it proceeds from principles given in faith |
| 8 | Is it argumentative? | Yes—it argues from authorities (Scripture) and reasons |
| 10 | Does Scripture use metaphors? | Yes—fittingly, since we know through sense |

**Key passage** (I.1.8 ad 2):
> "This science can receive something from the philosophical sciences, not as though it needed them, but for the greater clarification of what is handed down in this science."

### God's Existence and Nature (qq. 2-26)

#### Q. 2: The existence of God (3 articles)

| Article | Question | Key Content |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| 1 | Is God's existence self-evident? | No—not to us, though it is in itself |
| 2 | Is God's existence demonstrable? | Yes—a posteriori from effects |
| **3** | **Does God exist?** | **THE FIVE WAYS** |

**Q. 2, Article 3** (The Five Ways)—perhaps the most famous passage:

1. **Ex motu** (from motion): Whatever is moved is moved by another → Unmoved Mover
2. **Ex ratione causae efficientis** (from efficient cause): Causes require a first → First Cause
3. **Ex possibili et necessario** (from contingency): Not all can be merely possible → Necessary Being
4. **Ex gradibus** (from degrees): Degrees imply a maximum → Maximum Being
5. **Ex gubernatione rerum** (from governance): Order requires intelligence → Intelligent Governor

> "And this all men call God."

#### Q. 3: Divine Simplicity (8 articles)

| Article | Question | Answer |
|---------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Is God a body? | No |
| 2 | Is He composed of matter and form? | No |
| 3 | Is He composed of essence and existence? | No—He IS His existence |
| 4 | Are essence and existence the same in God? | Yes—*in solo Deo essentia est idem quod esse* |
| 7 | Is God altogether simple? | Yes—no composition whatsoever |

**Key passage** (I.3.4 corp.):
> "Therefore, since in God there is no potentiality, it follows that in Him essence does not differ from existence. Therefore His essence is His existence."

#### Q. 4-11: Divine Perfections

| Question | Topic | Key Points |
|----------|-------|------------|
| 4 | God's perfection | God contains all perfections eminently |
| 5 | Goodness in general | Good = being qua desirable |
| 6 | God's goodness | God is goodness itself |
| 7 | Infinity | God is infinite (not limited by matter) |
| 8 | Omnipresence | God is everywhere by essence, presence, power |
| 9 | Immutability | God cannot change |
| 10 | Eternity | God's duration: *tota simul* (all at once) |
| 11 | Unity | There is only one God |

#### Q. 12: How God Is Known (13 articles)

| Article | Question | Key Teaching |
|---------|----------|--------------|
| 1 | Can any created intellect see God's essence? | Yes—by grace in beatific vision |
| 4 | Can we see it by natural power? | No—requires light of glory |
| 12 | Can we know God by natural reason in this life? | Yes—through creatures, not His essence |
| 13 | Does grace give higher knowledge than reason? | Yes—but still not vision |

#### Q. 13: Divine Names (12 articles)

**Crucial for understanding analogy**

| Article | Question | Answer |
|---------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Can we name God? | Yes—from what we know of creatures |
| 2 | Are names predicated substantially? | Some are (goodness, wisdom) |
| 5 | Are names said univocally of God and creatures? | No—*analogically* |
| 6 | Are they first said of creatures? | Logically yes, but priority of perfection is in God |
| 11 | Is "He Who Is" the most proper name? | Yes—Exodus 3:14 |

**Key passage** (I.13.5 corp.):
> "Names are predicated of God and creatures analogically... not in a purely equivocal sense nor in a purely univocal sense."

### Law (qq. 90-108)

**Perhaps the most influential section for ethics and political philosophy**

#### Q. 90: Essence of Law

> "Law is an ordinance of reason for the common good, promulgated by him who has care of the community." (I-II.90.4 corp.)

#### Q. 91: Kinds of Law

| Article | Type of Law | Definition |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| 1 | Eternal law | God's reason governing all things |
| 2 | Natural law | Participation of eternal law in rational creatures |
| 3 | Human law | Particular applications made by human reason |
| 4 | Divine law | Revealed law (Old and New) |

**Key passage** (I-II.91.2 corp.):
> "The natural law is nothing else than the rational creature's participation of the eternal law."

#### Q. 94: Natural Law

| Article | Question | Answer |
|---------|----------|--------|
| 2 | Does it contain many precepts? | Yes—but one first principle |
| 4 | Is it the same for all? | In principles yes; in conclusions, mostly |
| 5 | Can it change? | By addition, not subtraction |
| 6 | Can it be abolished from hearts? | In general precepts, no |

**Key passage** (I-II.94.2 corp.):
> "The first precept of the law is that good is to be done and pursued, and evil is to be avoided."

## Research Tips

1. **Always check the objections**: Thomas states opposing views at their strongest
2. **Read the sed contra**: Often a Scripture or authority quote
3. **The corpus is the main answer**: *Respondeo dicendum...*
4. **Replies often add nuance**: *Ad primum...* may clarify or qualify
5. **Cross-reference**: Thomas often treats topics in multiple places
6. **Check Supplement cautiously**: Earlier work, compiled posthumously
7. **Note the audience**: This was written for theological students, not beginners in modern sense
