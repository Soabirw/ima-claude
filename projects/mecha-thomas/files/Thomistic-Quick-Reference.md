```

~1230-39   Oblate at Monte Cassino
1239-44    University of Naples (Arts)
1244       Joins Dominicans (family kidnaps him for a year)
1245-52    Paris and Cologne under Albert the Great
1252-56    Bachelor/Master at Paris
1256-59    First Paris Regency
           │ Commentary on Sentences (In Sent.)
           │ De Ente et Essentia (DEE)
           │ De Veritate (DV)
           │
1259-68    Italian Period (Orvieto, Rome, Viterbo)
           │ SUMMA CONTRA GENTILES (SCG) ~1259-65
           │ Catena Aurea (Gospel commentaries)
           │ De Potentia, De Malo (disputed questions)
           │ SUMMA THEOLOGIAE begins 1265
           │
1268-72    Second Paris Regency (contentious!)
           │ Continuation of SUMMA THEOLOGIAE
           │ Aristotle commentaries (De Anima, Ethics, etc.)
           │ Controversial works against Averroists
           │
1272-73    Naples
           │ SUMMA THEOLOGIAE continues (Third Part)
           │ December 6, 1273: Mystical experience
           │ "All I have written seems like straw..."
           │
1274       Dies March 7 at Fossanova (en route to Council of Lyon)
           │
1277       Some propositions condemned at Paris (not Thomas specifically)
1323       Canonized by Pope John XXII
1567       Named Doctor of the Church (Doctor Angelicus)
1879       Leo XIII, Aeterni Patris—Thomism restored as Catholic philosophy
```

## Citation Quick Reference

### Summa Theologiae Format

```
ST [Part].[Question].[Article] [section]

Sections:
- s.c. = sed contra ("on the contrary")
- corp. = corpus ("body" of the article - the respondeo)
- ad 1 = reply to first objection
- ad 2 = reply to second objection (etc.)
- obj. 1 = first objection (rarely cited separately)

Examples:
ST I.2.3 corp.        — First Part, Q2, Art.3, body
ST I-II.94.2 ad 1     — Prima Secundae, Q94, Art.2, reply to obj. 1  
ST II-II.23.1 s.c.    — Secunda Secundae, Q23, Art.1, sed contra
ST III.75.1 corp.     — Third Part, Q75, Art.1, body
```

### Other Works Format

```
Work Abbreviation [division]

SCG I.13              — Summa Contra Gentiles, Book I, Chapter 13
DV 1.1 corp.          — De Veritate, Q1, Art.1, body
DP 3.7 ad 2           — De Potentia, Q3, Art.7, reply to obj. 2
DM 1.5                — De Malo, Q1, Art.5
DEE c.4               — De Ente et Essentia, Chapter 4
In Sent. II.d.3.q.1.a.2 — Sentences Commentary, Bk II, Dist.3, Q1, Art.2
Comp. c.1             — Compendium Theologiae, Chapter 1
In Meta. I, lect.1    — Commentary on Metaphysics, Book I, Lecture 1
```

## Key Thomistic Concepts

### Metaphysics

| Concept | Latin | Meaning |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Being** | *ens* | That which exists or can exist |
| **Existence/Act of Being** | *esse* | The act by which a thing is |
| **Essence** | *essentia/quidditas* | What a thing is |
| **Act** | *actus* | Perfection, actuality |
| **Potency** | *potentia* | Capacity to be/receive act |
| **Substance** | *substantia* | That which exists in itself |
| **Accident** | *accidens* | That which exists in another |
| **Matter** | *materia* | Principle of potency/individuation |
| **Form** | *forma* | Principle of act/specification |
| **Real Distinction** | *distinctio realis* | Essence ≠ existence in creatures |

### Divine Names and Attributes

| Concept | Latin | Meaning |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Subsistent Being Itself** | *ipsum esse subsistens* | God's unique mode of existence |
| **Divine Simplicity** | *simplicitas Dei* | No composition in God |
| **Pure Act** | *actus purus* | No potency in God |
| **Analogy** | *analogia* | Words apply to God and creatures similarly but differently |
| **Negative Theology** | *via negativa* | Knowing God by what He is not |
| **Eminence** | *via eminentiae* | Knowing God by supreme perfection |

### Epistemology

| Concept | Latin | Meaning |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Agent Intellect** | *intellectus agens* | Abstracts universals from phantasms |
| **Possible Intellect** | *intellectus possibilis* | Receives intelligible forms |
| **Phantasm** | *phantasma* | Sensory image in imagination |
| **Abstraction** | *abstractio* | Deriving universal from particular |
| **Species** | *species* | Form by which thing is known |
| **First Principles** | *prima principia* | Self-evident starting points |

### Ethics

| Concept | Latin | Meaning |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Natural Law** | *lex naturalis* | Participation of eternal law in rational creatures |
| **Eternal Law** | *lex aeterna* | God's providence governing all things |
| **Beatitude** | *beatitudo* | Perfect happiness; vision of God |
| **Virtue** | *virtus* | Habit disposing to good action |
| **Mean** | *medium* | Right measure between extremes |
| **Prudence** | *prudentia* | Right reason about things to be done |
| **Synderesis** | *synderesis* | Habit of first practical principles |
| **Conscience** | *conscientia* | Application of knowledge to action |

### Theology

| Concept | Latin | Meaning |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Grace** | *gratia* | God's free gift enabling supernatural life |
| **Nature** | *natura* | What a thing is by its own principles |
| **Supernatural** | *supernaturalis* | Beyond natural capacity |
| **Merit** | *meritum* | Claim to reward from good action |
| **Infused Virtue** | *virtus infusa* | Virtue given directly by God |
| **Acquired Virtue** | *virtus acquisita* | Virtue developed by practice |

## The Five Ways (ST I.2.3)

| Way | From | Through | To |
|-----|------|---------|---|
| **First** | Motion | Nothing moves itself | Unmoved Mover |
| **Second** | Efficient Causation | No infinite regress in causes | First Cause |
| **Third** | Contingency | Not all can be merely possible | Necessary Being |
| **Fourth** | Gradation | Degrees imply maximum | Maximum Being |
| **Fifth** | Governance | Order requires intelligence | Intelligent Designer |

*Note: These are not five separate gods but five converging paths to the one God.*

## Famous Passages

### On God's Existence

> "The existence of God can be proved in five ways." (ST I.2.3 corp.)

### On Divine Simplicity

> "God is absolutely simple." (ST I.3.7 corp.)
> "In God, essence and existence are the same." (ST I.3.4 corp.)

### On Analogy

> "Names are predicated of God and creatures analogically." (ST I.13.5 corp.)

### On Natural Law

> "The natural law is nothing else than the rational creature's participation in the eternal law." (ST I-II.91.2 corp.)

### On Beatitude

> "Final and perfect beatitude can consist only in the vision of the Divine Essence." (ST I-II.3.8 corp.)

### On Grace and Free Will

> "Free will is the cause of its own movement... but God moves the will without forcing it." (ST I-II.111.2 corp.)

### On the Incarnation

> "It was fitting that God should become man." (ST III.1.1 corp.)

### On the Eucharist

> "The substance of the bread is changed into the substance of Christ's body." (ST III.75.2 corp.)

## Common Research Pitfalls

### Watch For

| Issue | Example | Response |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **Incomplete citation** | "Thomas says being and essence differ" | Specify where: ST I.3.4? DEE c.4? |
| **Summa Supplement** | "Thomas teaches about marriage in ST III" | Supplement compiled by Reginald, not Thomas |
| **Averroist confusion** | "Thomas denies personal immortality" | Thomas *opposes* this; see De Unitate Intellectus |
| **Later Thomism as Thomas** | "Thomists hold that..." | Distinguish Thomas from Thomists |
| **Translation issues** | "Being" vs. "existence" | Check Latin: *ens*? *esse*? *essentia*? |
| **Contested interpretations** | "Thomas obviously means..." | Note where scholars disagree |

### Quality Control

- ✓ Cite specific questions, articles, and sections
- ✓ Note which part of the article (objections, sed contra, corpus, replies)
- ✓ Check Latin terms when precision matters
- ✓ Distinguish Thomas' own words from paraphrase
- ✓ Acknowledge interpretive debates
- ✓ Cross-reference when Thomas addresses the same topic elsewhere

## The Chestertonian Touch

Remember: Present Thomistic truth with Chestertonian joy.

**Don't write**: "Thomas' doctrine of analogy allows meaningful predication of God despite divine transcendence."

**Do write**: "Thomas discovered the wild and wonderful fact that we can actually *talk* about God without either lying or going mad. The doctrine of analogy is not a compromise between atheism and anthropomorphism; it is the announcement that language, like everything else, was made by God—and therefore, however strange it seems, it reaches Him."
