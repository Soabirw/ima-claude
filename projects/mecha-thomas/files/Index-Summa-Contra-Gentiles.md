# Index: Summa Contra Gentiles

## Purpose

The **Summa Contra Gentiles** (1259-1265) is Thomas' other great summa—a systematic defense and exposition of Christian truth written for those engaged in evangelization and apologetics. Unlike the *Summa Theologiae*, which assumes faith and proceeds to understand, the *SCG* argues primarily from reason, adding revelation only in Books III-IV where natural reason cannot reach.

This work is sometimes called the "philosophical summa" versus the "theological summa" of the *ST*.

---

## Historical Context

**Date**: ~1259-1265 (begun in Orvieto, continued in Rome)

**Occasion**: Traditionally said to be written at the request of Raymond of Peñafort for Dominican missionaries engaging Muslims and Jews in Spain. More recent scholarship questions this specific purpose, but the work clearly addresses intelligent non-Christians and philosophical opponents.

**Structure**: Four books, moving from what can be known by reason alone (Books I-III) to what requires revelation (Book IV).

**Latin Title**: *Liber de Veritate Catholicae Fidei contra errores infidelium* ("Book on the Truth of the Catholic Faith against the Errors of Unbelievers")

## Book I: God's Existence and Nature (102 chapters)

*What can be known about God by natural reason*

### Purpose and Method (cc. 1-9)

| Chapter | Topic | Key Content |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | The office of the wise | To order and to refute falsehood |
| 2 | Author's intention | Manifest the truth professed by Catholic faith |
| 3 | Mode of procedure | Use reason and authority |
| 4 | Truth of reason vs. faith | Some truths knowable by both |
| 5 | What exceeds reason is not opposed to it | Faith and reason cannot contradict |
| 7 | Truth revealed exceeds reason | Necessity of revelation |
| 8 | How divine truth is related to reason | Preambles vs. mysteries |
| 9 | Order of procedure | First, what reason can discover |

**Key passage** (SCG I.7.1):
> "The truth that the human reason is naturally endowed to know cannot be opposed to the truth of the Christian faith."

### God's Existence (cc. 10-13)

| Chapter | Content |
|---------|---------|
| 10 | Opinion that God's existence cannot be demonstrated |
| 11 | Refutation of that opinion |
| 12 | Opinion that it can only be held by faith |
| **13** | **Arguments proving God's existence** |

**Chapter 13**: Arguments from motion, efficient causes, possibility/necessity, grades of being, governance. Similar to the Five Ways but more developed.

## Book III: Divine Providence (163 chapters)

*God's government of creation, especially rational creatures*

This is the longest book, covering God's providence, human happiness, law, and the governance of the universe.

## Book IV: The Mysteries of Faith (97 chapters)

*What exceeds reason but is revealed by God*

This book treats doctrines that cannot be demonstrated by philosophy but are known through revelation: Trinity, Incarnation, Sacraments, Last Things.

### Method (cc. 1-2)

| Chapter | Content |
|---------|---------|
| 1 | New subject matter—mysteries | "There are some truths about God that exceed all the ability of human reason" |

| Chapters | Topic |

| 3-9 | Generation of the Son |

**Key passage** (SCG IV.11.1):

| Chapters | Topic |
|----------|-------|

## Comparison: SCG vs. ST

| Aspect | Summa Contra Gentiles | Summa Theologiae |
|--------|----------------------|------------------|
| **Audience** | Non-Christians, missionaries | Theology students |
| **Method** | Reason first, then revelation | Faith seeking understanding |
| **Structure** | Continuous chapters | Questions/Articles |
| **Length** | ~250,000 words | ~1,500,000 words |
| **Scope** | Selected topics | Comprehensive |
| **Tone** | Apologetic, demonstrative | Pedagogical, systematic |
| **Date** | 1259-1265 | 1265-1273 |

*The two works complement each other. SCG shows what reason can establish; ST shows how faith illuminates the whole.*

## Key Themes and Passages

### On Faith and Reason

> "The truth of faith surpasses the capacity of human reason... But what is naturally present in reason cannot be contrary to this truth." (SCG I.7)

### On Divine Simplicity

> "God is His own existence, not merely having existence, but being existence itself." (SCG I.22)

### On Creation

> "To create is to produce a thing into being according to its entire substance." (SCG II.16)

### On the Soul

> "The intellectual soul has existence through itself, and is not corrupted when the body is corrupted." (SCG II.79)

### On Beatitude

> "The ultimate end of man... is the vision of the Divine Essence." (SCG III.48)

### On the Trinity

> "We do not confess three Gods, but one God in Trinity of Persons." (SCG IV.26)

### On the Incarnation

> "It was fitting for God to become man." (SCG IV.54)

## Research Tips

1. **Note the book's structure**: Books I-III use primarily philosophical arguments; Book IV proceeds from faith
2. **Compare with ST**: The same topic may receive different treatment given the different audience
3. **Pay attention to opponents**: Thomas often responds to specific philosophical positions (Averroism, etc.)
4. **Long chapters**: SCG chapters are more discursive than the tightly structured ST articles
5. **Earliest major synthesis**: Written before ST; represents Thomas' earlier systematic treatment
6. **Apologetic purpose**: Arguments are designed to convince, not just expound
